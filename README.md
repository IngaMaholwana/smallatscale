They are the backbone of the kasi economy.

Individually, many of these businesses are too small to compete with established retail chains when it comes to purchasing power, supplier access, distribution and economies of scale.

But what happens when they stop operating in isolation?

What if small businesses could buy like big businesses, without having to become one?

That's the idea behind The Kasi Economy.

The Idea

The Kasi Economy is a collective procurement and supply-chain platform designed to connect South Africa's informal and independent businesses.

Inspired by the spirit of the stokvel, the platform allows independent businesses to come together, pool their purchasing requirements and collectively source products and resources directly from suppliers.

Think of it as their digital stokie (stokvel).

Instead of every spaza shop buying 10 bags of maize meal independently, several shops can combine their orders and purchase 100 bags together.

Instead of individual vendors negotiating from a position of limited purchasing power, they negotiate as a collective.

Instead of struggling to access wholesalers, farms, manufacturers and distributors individually, they gain a shared gateway into the supply chain.

Independent businesses. Collective purchasing power. Shared opportunities.

The Problem

South Africa's informal economy is full of entrepreneurs who understand their customers, their communities and their businesses.

However, operating independently creates limitations:

Limited purchasing power: Small order quantities often mean higher unit prices.

Supplier accessibility: Many small businesses lack direct relationships with manufacturers, farms and large distributors.

Fragmented demand: Businesses need similar products but purchase independently.

Logistics challenges: Transporting bulk goods economically is difficult when every business operates alone.

Limited negotiating power: Small businesses cannot always access the discounts and commercial terms available to established retail chains.

The problem isn't necessarily a lack of demand.

It's that demand is fragmented.

The Kasi Economy aims to bring that demand together.

The Solution

Create a shared digital marketplace where independent businesses can:

Discover products and wholesale suppliers.

Create or join collective buying groups.

Combine purchasing quantities with other businesses.

Reach minimum order quantities (MOQs).

Unlock better bulk pricing.

Coordinate payments for individual allocations.

Organise shared transportation, delivery and collection.

Track orders from supplier to business.

Suppliers gain access to consolidated demand and a network of businesses that would otherwise be difficult to reach individually.

Businesses gain access to better purchasing opportunities and a more coordinated supply chain.
